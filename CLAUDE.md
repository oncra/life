# life (oncra/life) — repo rules

- Public, open-source repo under the `oncra` GitHub organisation. **No AI attribution in commits, PRs, code comments or docs** (Oncra house rule). Externally the author is the project; do not write "Claude" or "Diane" anywhere in this repo.
- Code is Apache-2.0, `content/` is CC BY 4.0. Keep licence headers consistent.
- Method rule: a change to `src/lib/readings.ts` must come with the matching change in `content/spec.md`.
- Never a weighted sum across readings; `UNKNOWN` must carry a note.
- Prisma 7: migrations are hand-reviewed SQL in `prisma/migrations`; the app runs `prisma migrate deploy` on start. No `prisma db push` against a shared database.
- Hardware prices in `content/hardware.md` carry a check date and URL.
- Link rule: documents in `content/` are read on GitHub as well as on the site, so a link to a sibling document is `kit.md` and an image is `../public/img/x`; `src/lib/markdown.ts` rewrites both to the site's routes. Never write a site-absolute `/docs/...` or `/img/...` link in `content/` (GitHub answers 404). A route with no file behind it (a place page, `openapi.json`) gets a full `https://life.oncra.org/...` URL. `npm run check:links` verifies this.
- Deploy: `ssh admin@<host> 'bash -s' < deploy/deploy.sh` (clones/updates `/opt/life`, builds, `docker compose up -d`). Verify `https://life.oncra.org/api/health` afterwards.
- **Current pickup point**: the kit is `content/kit.md` (Life node v1, one solar 4G box, ~€826 with the 20 Ah battery, the €13 SIM, the bat microphone and the visit contact, about €80 a year over the SIM's ten years) with `kit/order-list.csv` and node software in `node/`. `kit/order-list.csv` is the source of truth for parts and prices; the table on the kit page describes it and must be changed with it.
- **Node 1 purchases are in `kit/node-1-purchases.csv`** (order numbers, totals, delivery status). As of 2026-09-24: reichelt, UUGear, TelecomShop delivered; Berrybase 1070372 cancelled by Berrybase (VAT number asked after checkout; never do that, enter it in the account first); 1NCE SIM shipped; Kiwi 817879 (Pi 4 4 GB, RS485, card, small parts) shipped; DigiKey 101831753 (4x SEN0600) processing; Amazon.nl (PSU, USB-C meter, mics, terminal adapters, lacquer, shrink, ties, tape) arriving 26 Sep; AliExpress membrane 12-24 Oct. **Not yet ordered: Accuweb panel, reichelt battery, bouwmarkt run.** The bench (stages 0-1 of `content/build.md`) needs none of those.
- **Listening schedule (2026-10-12, one window length since 2026-10-13)** (`content/spec.md` 2.2): the same at every place, every day of a season and every year, and the only hours the readings count: Mar-Oct 4 h from civil dawn + 1 h from sunset, Nov-Feb 2 h from civil dawn (from sunrise until 2026-11-04: a winter morning peaks 30 to 40 min before sunrise, and a 2 h window hears the most species from civil dawn). The node listens to exactly these windows (no extra hours; until 13 Oct the dawn window was 6 h in May-Jul and the node added battery-conditional extra hours). Node side: `node/image/life-schedule-wpi.py` writes them into the Witty Pi (`--windows` prints them as JSON with the next start); `node/image/life-window.py` (run at the end of `life-schedule`) sends a node the Witty Pi woke by itself outside its windows (battery recovered, load output back) back to sleep after its post, with the next start set to the next window, while a boot by hand (button, reboot, USB) stays on; its decision is `/data/life-node/window.json` and rides on the heartbeat as `window` / `offWindow`. Oracle side: `src/lib/listening.ts` (`coreWindows`, `inCore`, `coreWindowArrays`, civil dawn from `sunAt` in sun.ts; the names keep `core`) filters the detections `computeReadings` passes to diversity, renewal and autonomy, and the life index's species lane; every other view shows all detections. Changing the windows is a new method version. Why: BirdWeather replay of four Dutch 24 h stations and an hour-by-hour PVGIS battery simulation, summarised in the spec. The 20 Ah battery became standard on 2026-10-12.
- **Battery guard (2026-10-13, image-v6):** `node/image/life-wittypi.sh` sets the Witty Pi's own low-voltage stop and recovery level at every boot (`WITTYPI_LOW_V` 12.6, `WITTYPI_RECOVERY_V` 13.2; registers 19 and 22, tenths of a volt, 255 = off; written only when changed). Both are needed: with only the low level the board skips a scheduled start while the battery is low and that alarm does not return for a month; a recovery level alone keeps switching the Pi on. `WITTYPI_LOW_V=0` (mains adapter) turns both off. The heartbeat carries `lowV`, `recoveryV`, `lvOff`, and `wake` = `low-voltage` on the way down after a guard stop; the oracle opens LOW_BATTERY under 12.8 V or on such a shutdown and closes it at 13.2 V (`src/lib/battery.ts`). Why: node 1's 10 Ah ran into its BMS on 12 Oct with only the Victron's load disconnect as a guard.
- **Node liveness and theft are software (2026-09-25):** `node/life-heartbeat.py` rides on the hourly push (`LIFE_HEARTBEAT_CMD`) with metrics and the 4G stick's serving cell; `src/lib/alerts.ts` keeps the first cell as `Device.homeCell`, opens `MOVED` on a cell change and `SILENT` after 36 h (worker sweep, hourly), and pushes to `ALERT_WEBHOOK_URL` (set on the oracle host to the console's notify route). Steward routes: `GET/POST /places/{id}/alerts[/{alertId}]`. No Bluetooth tracker in the kit; the AirTag is an owner-maintained `alt` row. **Tamper guard (2026-09-25):** `node/firmware/life-guard` (ATtiny84 sketch + hex + flash.sh, lid/panel reeds + tilt, 12 V siren, 60 s lid entry delay), `node/life-guard.py` (Pi side: `check` at boot, `apply` from `life-push`, disarm only with a maintenance window), oracle `TAMPER` alert + `PUT/GET/DELETE /places/{id}/devices/{deviceId}/maintenance`, heartbeat answers carry `maintenance`. **Out of the standard node since 2026-11-02** (Sven: electronics as simple as possible); code stays, off unless `GUARD=1`. The standard node has the **simple alarm** instead (`GUARD=simple`, 2026-11-02; live wake 2026-11-03). **Its circuit, board layout and build instructions are deliberately not in this public repo** (Sven, 2026-11-03: theft protection is not shared openly): keep content/, kit/ notes, code comments, PR and commit text at the level of "a small board without a chip, siren on panel removal and tilt, wakes the node, Pi reads GPIO 10". Software contract: `life-window` treats a button wake with the alarm standing as no visit (`offWindow: alarm`, power off after `ALARM_S` 40 min unless a maintenance window); `life-guard` reports `event: alarm`, loop `unknown`, with retries at boot (`REPORT_TRIES`), keeps the answer's maintenance window, and rechecks every 5 min for 30 min for cell tracking; it never silences the alarm. Build stage 3.7 with `public/img/life-alarm.svg`. Basket `R2` = visit contact + alarm parts.

Every `##` is a step, numbered across the whole guide; a blockquote starting `**Check.**` becomes the step's check card, which for the desk steps is a light on the builder's `/box` page (`boxLive` in `src/lib/boxout.ts`). Old stage URLs (`/lifebox/1-bench` ..., now `/node/1-bench`) redirect to the part's anchor (`legacyAnchors`). Images follow the GitHub rule `../../public/img/x`. Feedback on the page lands in `PlanFeedback` (`/api/lifebox/feedback`, page `index`); an `AI_CHANGE` needs no code (2026-10-01: honeypot, five per visitor per hour, thirty a day) and is picked up within a minute by a runner outside this repo (claude-remote `box/cron/lifebox-ai.sh`), which applies it and then puts a "roll back?" card in the maintainer's consent queue; a rollback reverts the merge, redeploys and marks the item `ROLLED_BACK`. The runner edits only `content/lifebox/`, `kit/bench-log.csv` and `kit/node-1-purchases.csv`. The technical record stays in `content/build.md` (/docs/build).

A change under `node/` that should reach nodes means a rebuild and a new release; the plan pages link the `latest` asset, so the asset names must not change.

- **Per-box images at `/box` (2026-10-01):** people sign in with a six-digit code (Brevo HTTP API, `BREVO_API_KEY`; Brevo blocks API calls from IPs not on its authorised list, so a new oracle host IP must be added there first), name a box, give the WiFi, and download a card image made for it. `src/lib/boximage.ts` keeps the golden image split at the root partition in `/opt/life/images/golden` (`node/image/split-golden.sh`; head raw, tail xz, `VERSION`), writes the box's `life-node.env` into the FAT with mtools and serves head + tail as two xz streams in one download (~30 s per box). A box gets a private place with `placed=false` (worker skips it) and three devices at once; the location is set at placement. New software for a box mints new tokens. **A new golden image only reaches /box after `split-golden.sh` and an upload to `/opt/life/images/golden`.**
- **Location privacy (2026-10-01):** a place with `locationHidden` shows the public a circle (`blurRadiusM`, default 1 km) around a centre drawn once and stored (`blurLat/blurLon`), never redrawn per request. Exact boundary, centroid, soil-context coordinates and device positions go only to the place's Box owner (signed in), its steward key, admin keys and `ADMIN_EMAILS`; everything goes through `src/lib/privacy.ts` (`canView`, `canSeeExact`, `publicPlace`). Private places (`public=false`) 404 for everyone else. Box places hide their location by default. A new route that returns place geometry or coordinates must go through `publicPlace`.
- **Species pictures (2026-10-12):** the node dashboard opens with the last five species heard as cut-outs (`LastHeard`, data `lastHeard()` in `src/lib/node.ts`). Model `SpeciesImage` (one row per detector species name; `image` is a WebP with alpha, longest side 720 px; author, licence and the Commons file page travel with it; `status` pending/ready/failed). The worker's hourly sweep (`speciesImageSweep` in `src/lib/species-image.ts`) finds the lead photo of the species' English Wikipedia article (scientific name first), reads the Commons credits, removes the background with U²-Net (`u2net.onnx` from the rembg release, sha256 pinned, fetched once to `IMAGE_DIR/models`; the worker mounts `./images` for it) through `onnxruntime-node`, and stores the result; a failure is retried after thirty days. Served by `GET /api/species-image/{id}.webp` with a week of cache. onnxruntime-node ships 290 MB of binaries; the Dockerfile keeps the Linux ones. Credits are shown under the row (CC BY-SA and friends require it). Nothing runs at view time. **A species is its scientific name** (2026-10-12): BirdNET-Go's database has no common names, so `clients/birdnet-pi-push.py` sometimes sends the Latin as `species`; everything that counts or lists species groups by `coalesce(scientific, species)` (`diversity()` in readings.ts, life-index.ts, `speciesTable` and `heard()` in node.ts) and names the row by a common name if any detection or `SpeciesImage` of that bird has one. `SpeciesImage.names` holds common names by language (en, nl, de, fr, es, zh) from Wikidata P1843 (`wikidataNames`, filled with the picture and by `speciesNameSweep` in the same hourly worker run; `{}` = none found). **Open data at `GET /api/v1/heard`** (`?limit=`, `?place=`, `?min=` default 0.7, `?images=only`; CORS open, a minute of cache): the newest species with picture URL, credits, names and node, plus `nodes[]` with listening/asleep/quiet/silent. The climatecleanup.org home page shows it live (repo svenjense/climatecleanup-org-v5, block `lifeOracle`), so a change to its shape needs that block checked. When the per-place switch for the public node dashboard is built (backlog), this endpoint must follow it.
- **Hand-built nodes on `/box` (2026-10-12):** devices that have reported but belong to no box made on `/box` (notes not `Life Box <hostname>`, e.g. node 1's `bench` devices) show as a `NodeCard` on `/box` for the people who look after that place (PlaceAccess, or every place for an `ADMIN_EMAILS` address): the same lights, the link to `/places/{slug}/node`, no software steps. `handNodes(user)` in `src/lib/boxout.ts`; `boxLive(placeId, undefined, true)` picks only those devices. Moving a hand-built node to another place is a `Device.placeId` change, not a new box.
- **A box on an existing place (2026-10-01):** someone with `PlaceAccess` to a place can set a box up for it at `/box` ("Where it will stand"). Such a box has `ownPlace=false`: its three devices are created on that place (marked `notes = "Life Box <hostname>"`, which is how `buildImage`, `boxLive` and `placeBox` find a box's own devices on a shared place), the image carries the place's centroid from the start, and placing it only records the device positions (the spot must be within 200 m of the boundary); the boundary is never redrawn.
- **Backlog at `/backlog` (2026-10-08):** a kanban (Backlog, Next, Doing, Done) of ideas met while building, model `BacklogCard` (`lane`, float `rank`, lowest on top). `rankFor` in `src/lib/backlog.ts` places a card at `top` / `bottom` / `{before}` / `{after}` with the midpoint between its neighbours and renumbers the lane when halving runs out of room; the client mirrors it (`moved` in `src/components/Backlog.tsx`) so a move shows at once. Everyone reads it. Changing it takes the board password (`BACKLOG_PASSWORD` in the host's `.env`, never in this repo; typed once it leaves the `life_backlog` cookie, a hash that changes with the password, so a new password locks every browser out), any sign-in, or the admin key. API: `GET/POST /api/backlog`, `PATCH/DELETE /api/backlog/{id}` (`{lane, at}` moves; a lane change without `at` goes on top), `POST/DELETE /api/backlog/unlock`.
- **Node dashboard at `/places/{slug}/node` (2026-10-11):** what a node measured and how it was doing over time. It opens with the **life index** (2026-10-12; `src/lib/life-index.ts` + `src/components/node/LifeIndexChart.tsx`, own range `?index=30d|1y|archive`): the verdict per quarter from the stored `Reading` rows through `verdict()` (never a sum; fewer than seven readings = insufficient) above the three lanes it is read from: `SatelliteObs` per scene with growing seasons shaded, species per local day (confidence ≥ 0.7, as `diversity()` counts), daily means of the shallowest probe with the cycling reading's breathing formula (`soilActivity`, keep equal to `cycling()`). The method itself lives in `readings.ts` / `content/spec.md`; the chart only shows it. Then: the heartbeat strip (every post a tick; a silence longer than the three-hour rhythm, `POST_EVERY_H`, is a band, *asleep* after a shutdown, *missed* otherwise), soil moisture and temperature per probe, battery, box and processor temperature and power from the heartbeat metrics, species counts (today, seven days, all) and a dawn-chorus grid (detections per local hour, last fourteen days), and the map with the node as a pulsing signal, folded in a `Collapsible` under the globe (mounted only when open, so Leaflet measures a real size; zoom 8 for a blurred place, 13 for an exact one). The hero is a white panel in the site's own colours with a **globe** (`src/components/node/Globe.tsx`, canvas 2D, no library): the Earth as rows of dots from `src/lib/globe-dots.ts` (generated by `scripts/globe-dots.mjs` from Natural Earth 110m land, public domain; rerun it only to change the density), every node glowing with rings while it is heard, and its posts as pulses of light travelling along an arc to the oracle in Nuremberg (`ORACLE` in the component); it breathes around the focus node and can be dragged (the ground follows the finger). Data in `src/lib/node.ts` (`nodeData`, `nodeSignals`, `signalPoint`), also served as `GET /api/v1/places/{id}/node?range=24h|7d|30d|all` and `GET /api/v1/nodes`; the charts are hand-drawn SVG in `src/components/node/` (no chart library), the signals are `LifeMap`'s `signals` / `fetchSignals` (CSS `.sig` in globals.css). **Public for every place, also a private one** (decided 2026-10-11): it says nothing about where the node stands. The cell id in the heartbeat is never shown, only the operator and the signal strength; the map point goes through `signalPoint`, which gives the exact centroid only to viewers who may see the place exactly, the stored blurred centre for a place that hides its location, and for a private place without one the centroid snapped to a 0.1 degree grid (about 7 km), the same answer every time. Times are shown in `DISPLAY_ZONE` (`src/lib/clock.ts`, Europe/Amsterdam until a node stands elsewhere); night bands come from sunrise and sunset at the place (`src/lib/sun.ts`). The clock helpers build labels from numeric parts on purpose: Intl's own date strings differ between the server's and a browser's ICU and would break hydration.
- **Why a Life Node (2026-10-20):** the node's selling points are one list, `src/components/NodeWhy.tsx` (`NODE_LEAD`, `NODE_SCALE`, six cards), shown on the home page in the Life Node section (`#life-node`, moved up to sit right under the three streams it delivers) and on `/node` under the intro, because `lifenodes.org` lands there. The core point is autonomy, and through it scale: own power, own 4G, no visits, so the next node is one more post. Every line is a fact about the node as built (battery guard levels, the €13 ten-year SIM, the alerts, the kit price): when the node changes, change the list. The price comparison behind "how it compares" is `content/kit.md`, "How it compares", with shop, URL and check date per line.
- **Two microphones and their check (2026-10-29, oncra/life#134):** the standard node (`LIFE_BIRD_MIC=audiomoth`, now the default) hears birds and bats on the AudioMoth and keeps the I2S microphone beside it by day as `I2S witness` (BirdNET only; `clients/birdnet-pi-push.py` always marks that source test). `node/life-mic.py` gives no source quiet hours (in BirdNET-Go 20261014 one sound card in quiet hours stops every sound card); sources and models switch with the sun. While two microphones hear birds, BirdNET-Go's shared species pause `realtime.interval` is 0 (#133). The witness listens only on the check day, once a week (`LIFE_MIC_CHECK_DAY`, Sunday; every day until `/data/life-node/mic-checks.json` holds five check days of 10+ readings), and in mode `both` every day. On those days it switches on BirdNET-Go's sound levels and its loopback metrics page (`realtime.audio.soundlevel`, `realtime.telemetry` 127.0.0.1:9090); `life-mic sync` runs `levels` every five minutes while the node is awake, which reads both microphones' 1/3 octave levels there (source id = `audio_card_` + 8 hex of sha256 of the device string), keeps them three days in `/data/life-node/mic-levels/` and writes `/data/life-node/mics.json` (per band: AudioMoth minus I2S, each level, each floor). `node/life-heartbeat.py` sends it as `metrics.mics` with the first post after it changed. Oracle: `src/lib/mics.ts` (`micVerdict`, checks in `npm run mics:test`) against the node's own check days of the last 70 days (judged from four); `checkMics` in alerts.ts opens `MIC` (6 dB in two bird bands, or a microphone without levels) and resolves within 3 dB.
- **Clips and visits (2026-11-02, oncra/life#141):** a node keeps every clip from 0.5 up while `/data` has 2 GB free (`LIFE_CLIPS_ALL_FROM`, `LIFE_CLIPS_MIN_FREE_MB`), the five-per-band sample below that, each cut on the node to 7 s of AAC (ffmpeg is in `provision.sh`). Clips never go over the SIM: `life-clips upload` sends them over a cable or a WiFi (`POST /api/v1/clips`, deleted on the SHA-256 echo); a visit (`node/life-visit.py`, `LIFE_VISIT=1`) starts on the Witty Pi's button or a magnet on the visit contact (reed on GPIO 22 = header pin 15, `LIFE_VISIT_PIN`, kit row 22b, build step 3.8). The contact's capacitor makes a magnet one press and its second transistor blocks it while VOUT is high, because the Witty Pi 4 firmware acts on the press itself, shuts the node down on a press while it is up, and cuts the power after 7 s held. The node joins the owner's hotspot (set on the node page, default `hotspot` / `12345678`, names matched across case, spaces and apostrophes) or opens its own WiFi `life-<last 6 of the device id>` (10.42.0.1, no gateway or DNS, nft table `life_visit`) for the Life Clips app (`clients/clips-app/`, Go, released on tags `clips-app-v*` and never marked latest), which uploads with a grant (`src/lib/grant.ts`) and `POST /api/v1/clips/batches`; the node deletes those clips when a heartbeat answer names the batch. With visits on, a button press outside the windows powers the node off after the visit unless a maintenance window is open (`life-window`). The magnet never silences the guard. The meadow-bird window (2026-11-02) is an owner's option, not a window of the method: off by default, set on the node dashboard (`src/lib/night.ts` the rule, `src/lib/node-night.ts` settings and state, `NightWindow.tsx`, `/api/v1/places/{id}/devices/{deviceId}/night`), sent as `night` in the heartbeat answer when the node's `nightRev` differs, kept by `life-window apply` in `night.json` and scheduled by `life-schedule-wpi`; `listening.ts` never counts it. The node and `night.ts` must keep the same rule (after the last core window, at least 15 minutes before the next first, under 15 minutes no window); `npm run night:test` and `node/tests/test_life_schedule_wpi.py` (needs astral) check each side.
- **Heat and the bats' gates (2026-11-03):** `node/life-heat.py` (service) samples the SoC every 15 s into `/run/life-node/heat.json` and trips a guard at `LIFE_BATS_MAX_C` (78, hysteresis 8 K, at least 15 min out) that starts `life-mic-sync`; `life-mic` keeps the bat model out for `heat`, or for the evening when the Witty Pi reads under `LIFE_BATS_MIN_V` (13.1, `LIFE_BATS_BATTERY=1`; under 6 V is a 5 V supply and keeps the bats) as the bat hours begin, and accounts the evening's minutes in `LIFE_STATE/bats.json` (onS/heatS/batteryS). `night` keeps meaning bat time (no witness, no sound levels) when the model is gated. `life-clips` defers cutting and sending while bats run or the SoC is at 70 °C or more; `cut`/`upload --now` for visits. Heartbeat: `throttled` every post; `heat`, `bats`, `overruns` at shutdown or when something is wrong, `bng` (BirdNET-Go's version) on boot and shutdown posts. `boxTempC` is the Witty Pi's sensor above the SoC, not box air.
- **Settings over the air (2026-11-02, oncra/life#146):** `node/life-config.py` takes a node's settings from the oracle's answer to a heartbeat. The node reports `cfgRev` (empty for none yet; nothing on a node without life-config, which is then sent nothing); `configFor` in `src/lib/node-config.ts` sends `config` {rev, windows, env} only when that differs from the current rev and the node has not refused it (`cfgRefused`). `windows` are the method's rules, `METHOD_RULES` in `src/lib/listening.ts`, the same at every node, so a new version of the method reaches every node without a visit; `coreWindows` stays the counting, and `npm run config:test` holds the two to each other on every day of two years from 55° S to 70° N, and `NODE_SETTINGS` to `SETTINGS` in life-config.py and `METHOD_RULES` to `METHOD` in life-schedule-wpi.py. Change a window rule, a setting or a bound on both sides at once. `env` is the owner's values (`Device.config`, `configN` counts saves so a save always gives a new rev), set on the node dashboard (`NodeSettings`) or `PUT /places/{id}/devices/{deviceId}/config`. The node checks everything again: unknown keys left out (`cfgIgnored`), a value out of bounds refuses the set (`cfgWhy`), the battery guard never off from here and refused on a mains node or above the input now; new settings on trial, and when three whole wakes after them went unanswered the next wake starts on the last good ones (`life-config boot`, first in life-schedule.sh; the count is of wakes that are over). Env values go into a block at the end of `/data/life-node/env` that only life-config writes; new windows go in at once via `life-schedule again`. The heartbeat carries `settings` (what the node runs) once after each change. The meadow-bird window keeps its own channel (`night`, `nightRev`). Never put anything that can cut the link (SIM, network, tokens, oracle URL, SSH, software) on this list.
