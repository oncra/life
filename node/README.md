# Life node v1: one box, one post, 4G

A single solar-powered node that hears (BirdNET on a Raspberry Pi 4; the Pi Zero 2 W would halve the power draw but is out of stock in the EU until December 2026 and BirdNET-Go dropped support for it), reads two wired soil probes (RS485) and posts JSON to the oracle over LTE-M. No farm network, no LoRaWAN gateway, no WiFi access point. Bill of materials and prices: `/docs/kit`.

Software on the node:
- **BirdNET-Go** (or BirdNET-Pi) does the listening and species detection; only detections leave the node, so a 500 MB ten-year IoT SIM is enough.
- `life-soil-agent.py` reads the probes over Modbus RTU every 20 minutes into a queue on `/data`. On the node it does not post (`LIFE_SOIL_VIA_PUSH=1`); run on its own it posts to `/api/v1/ingest/soil` itself.
- `life-push` (= `clients/birdnet-pi-push.py`) is the node's one way out: three minutes after boot, every three hours after that and once on the way down, it posts new detections to `/api/v1/ingest/sound` with the node's heartbeat on the same post, and then the soil queue to `/api/v1/ingest/soil`, all over one connection. Counted on node 1, a post on a connection of its own costs 9 to 10 kB, of which the TLS handshake is about 6.5; sound and two probes separately came to 28 kB an hour, twice what a 500 MB ten-year SIM allows.
- `life-heartbeat.py` builds that heartbeat: uptime, CPU temperature, free space on `/data`, unsent queue length, battery voltage, the temperature inside the box and what the Pi draws (from the Witty Pi, plus panel and charge figures from the controller when a VE.Direct cable is in), and the cell the 4G stick is camped on (PLMN and cell id from the stick's own HiLink web API). The oracle keeps the first cell heard as the node's home cell and opens a **moved** alert when a later heartbeat comes from another one, and a **silent** alert when a node that has been heard goes quiet for 36 hours. That is the theft answer: no coin-cell tracker, no extra hardware, nothing that chirps. A stolen box that is powered anywhere reports the cell it landed in on its next scheduled wake.
- `firmware/life-guard/` is the tamper guard, not in the standard node since 2026-11-02 (theft protection is security bolts plus the oracle's moved and silent alerts), kept for a site that wants an alarm: an ATtiny84 on the Witty Pi's always-on 3V3 pin with two reed contacts (lid, panel), a tilt switch and a 12 V piezo siren; `life-guard.py` is the Pi's side of it. The lid gets a sixty-second entry delay with chirps while the Pi boots and asks the oracle; panel and tilt get the siren at once. The guard is silenced only by a pulse from the Pi, and the Pi only sends it with a maintenance window in hand, from the oracle's answer or from a window cached at an earlier heartbeat. `life-guard.service` runs `life-guard check` first thing at every boot; `life-guard-watch.service` runs the same check the moment the guard alarms while the Pi is already up, which the guard itself cannot signal; `life-push` feeds each heartbeat answer to `life-guard apply`. `GUARD=1` in the env when the board is fitted.
- `life-mic.py` serves the microphones, the AudioMoth USB Microphone and the I2S one. At every boot it sets the AudioMoth to 192 kHz, finds the sound cards and writes BirdNET-Go's sources for the node's mode, `LIFE_BIRD_MIC`: `i2s` (birds on the I2S microphone, the AudioMoth a bat microphone that runs only the bat model, from fifteen minutes before sunset until shortly before civil dawn; no quiet hours, which in BirdNET-Go stop every sound card), `audiomoth` (the AudioMoth the one microphone: the bird models all the time, the bat model from fifteen minutes before sunset until just before civil dawn) or `both` (the two side by side, to compare them: BirdNET alone on the AudioMoth by day, the bat model alone at night; BirdNET-Go's 15-second pause per species, which all its sources share, goes to 0 so each microphone keeps its own detections). `audiomoth` is the default since 29 October 2026, with the I2S microphone beside the AudioMoth by day as its witness once a week (`I2S witness`, always sent as test; every day for a new node's first five check days) and the two microphones' sound levels compared on that day (`life-mic levels`, carried as `mics` on the heartbeat, MIC alert on the oracle). A timer moves the bat model with the sun on a node that stays on. Without the AudioMoth the node listens on the I2S microphone (`image/README.md`, The AudioMoth).
- `life-clips.py` keeps clips on the card for checking and research (`content/spec.md`, 2.2): every clip from a score of 0.5 up while `/data` has more than 2 GB free (`LIFE_CLIPS_ALL_FROM`, `LIFE_CLIPS_MIN_FREE_MB`), below that at most five per species, month and score band of 0.1; bird and bat detections inside the listening windows only, voices filtered out by BirdNET-Go, every other clip deleted within half an hour. Each kept clip is cut with ffmpeg to 7 seconds from the start of the detection, levelled and stored as AAC, a bat clip slowed down ten times. Nothing goes over 4G. While the node's way out is a cable or a WiFi, `life-clips upload` (`life-clips.service`, after each sample run) sends the clips to `/api/v1/clips` and deletes each one when the oracle answers with its SHA-256 (`LIFE_CLIPS_UPLOAD=0` leaves that to visits). At a visit (`life-visit.py`, below) they leave the same way over the visitor's hotspot, or onto a laptop, and those are deleted when the oracle's answer to a heartbeat names their batch (`life-clips apply`). A USB stick named LIFECLIPS (FAT32 or exFAT) plugged in at a visit still takes a copy with a list (`clips.csv`), the number of detections each month's clips were drawn from per species and score band (`bands.csv`, the weights for the calibration) and checksums, the copy is read back, and only then is the sample deleted from the node; the green light stays on for ten seconds when it worked and blinks when it did not. `LIFE_KEEP_CLIPS=0` turns it off and deletes every clip; `LIFE_CLIPS_ANYTIME=1` keeps clips outside the windows, for a bench. `life-clips status` says what is waiting, and the heartbeat carries it (`clipsWaiting`, `clipsMb`, `clipsFull`, `clipsCollectedAt`, `clipsSentAt`). Tests: `python3 -B -m unittest discover -s node/tests`.
- `life-visit.py` runs a visit (`LIFE_VISIT=1`), started by a magnet held for three seconds on the visit contact (a reed read on GPIO 22, `LIFE_VISIT_PIN`, which also wakes a sleeping node through the Witty Pi's button input; the kit page's row 22b) or by a press of the Witty Pi's button. The node joins the visitor's phone hotspot, with the name and password its owner sets on the node's page ("hotspot" and 12345678 until then), sends its clips and posts. Without a hotspot within 90 seconds, or with clips left, it opens its own WiFi (`life-` and the last six characters of its id, the password on its page) for the Life Clips app on a laptop (`../clients/clips-app/`), which takes the clips with a grant the node signs for exactly those clips and sends them to the oracle once it is online. That WiFi leads nowhere: its DHCP answers carry no gateway and no name server, and an nftables table admits DHCP, the clip server and a ping and nothing else. On a hotspot a table of the same name lets in only the answers to the node's own connections. A visit lasts at most 30 minutes (`VISIT_MAX_MIN`); one that woke the node ends with it asleep until its next window (`image/life-window.py`), unless the owner has a maintenance window open. `life-visit-watch.service` reads the contact while the node is up, and `life-push` hands each heartbeat answer to `life-visit apply`, which takes the hotspot and the node's own WiFi password from it. `apply-visit.sh on 22` puts visits on a running node; with `--install`, and only on the cable or the WiFi, it also installs ffmpeg, nftables and dnsmasq-base where they are missing. `life-visit status` says how it stands.
- `life-tailscale.py` makes a node on the bench reachable from anywhere, not only from its own network: with `LIFE_TAILSCALE=1` it joins its steward's tailnet ([Tailscale](https://tailscale.com), in userspace networking, so no routes, firewall rules or DNS change on the node; SSH that comes in over the tailnet goes to the node's own sshd). The SIM never carries it: tailscaled runs only while the way out to the internet is the cable or the WiFi and stops the moment it is anything else, because an idle Tailscale keeps a connection to its coordination server and a relay open all day, traffic the SIM budget has no room for. Where systemd has its BPF framework (Debian 13 on the Pi does) the kernel also refuses tailscaled every interface but the cable, the WiFi and localhost (`RestrictNetworkInterfaces=`). Its log upload to Tailscale is off. `apply-tailscale.sh on <build>` puts it on a running node (Tailscale's static build, fetched on a laptop and never over 4G), and `apply-tailscale.sh forget` takes the node out of the tailnet before it goes to a field, deleting its identity so a stolen node cannot rejoin. `life-tailscale status` says which way out the node has and whether Tailscale may use it. SSH that arrives over the tailnet takes keys only (it reaches sshd from localhost, `image/sshd-life.conf`), and a second key that survives a reboot goes in `/data/ssh/authorized_keys.life`: `life-firstboot` writes `~/.ssh/authorized_keys` anew from `NODE_SSH_KEY` at every boot. That is how a server in the steward's tailnet fetches the clip sample for the triage page every quarter of an hour (`../clients/lifeclips-upload.service`): as its own user, with no sudo and no view of the server's other files, reaching only the node and the oracle, with a key the node accepts only over the tailnet and without a terminal (`restrict,from="127.0.0.1,::1"`).
- `provision.sh` installs all of it onto a Raspberry Pi OS Lite root, and `image/build.sh` runs it inside a stock image on a workstation to make the golden image: read-only overlay root, a writable `/data` partition for everything that must persist, BirdNET-Go with clip saving off, the Witty Pi daemon and the season's schedule. Per-node settings (tokens, hostname, coordinates) go on the boot partition as `life-node.env` after flashing; see `image/README.md`.

The schedule is one rule for every node and every year (`content/spec.md`, section 2.2), and the only part of the day the oracle's readings count: from March to October **4 hours from civil dawn and 1 hour from sunset**, from November to February **2 hours from sunrise**. The node listens to exactly these windows, the same length on every day of a season (until 13 October 2026 the dawn window was 6 hours in May to July and a node added extra hours when its battery allowed; one length is easier to read and leaves the battery more slack). The windows are computed on the node for its own place at every boot (`image/life-schedule-wpi.py`), and every heartbeat says which window the node woke for (`window`). **The battery guards itself.** The Witty Pi shuts the Pi down cleanly when its input falls below `WITTYPI_LOW_V` (12.6 V), well above the charge controller's own hard cut, and switches it on again above `WITTYPI_RECOVERY_V` (13.2 V), which on a node means the panel is charging; `image/life-wittypi.sh` sets both at every boot. A node the board woke by itself outside its windows (the battery back after such a stop, or power back on the load output) posts and powers off again ten minutes after boot until the next window (`image/life-window.py`); a boot by hand stays on. Why these hours: a year of four Dutch 24-hour BirdNET stations replayed against every schedule, and the battery simulated hour by hour on PVGIS weather for 2005 to 2023. The soil timer keeps its 20-minute interval; in winter that gives six readings in the two hours the node is awake, plenty for soil that moves slowly.

**Probe addresses set themselves.** Both probes ship on Modbus address 1. With `PROBE_ADDRESSES=1,2`, the first time the soil agent finds exactly one probe on 1 and none on 2, it moves that probe to 2 and writes `/data/life-node/probe-2-set`, so it happens once per card. The build guide has the builder connect the 30 cm probe alone first, then add the 10 cm one. `PROBE_AUTO_ADDRESS=0` turns it off; `--set-address` still works by hand.

The probes take 5 V from a USB-A breakout on the Pi, so they are powered exactly when the Pi is: the Witty Pi switches them for free. Do not wire them to the charge controller's load output, which is never itself switched; there they would draw around 0.5 W all day and night, more than the whole winter budget. (`PROBE_POWER_GPIO` still exists for a build that wants a MOSFET-switched 12 V rail instead.)

**No sound leaves the node over its SIM.** Detections do; the clips leave only over a cable or a WiFi the node is on, at a visit over the visitor's phone hotspot or onto a laptop, or on a stick, and never with a voice in them: BirdNET-Go drops any detection in which a voice is heard, clip and all. On the oracle they go to the [triage page](https://life.oncra.org/triage), where anyone may listen and signed-in people judge each clip, and the sound is deleted after 30 days unless the place's owner sets a different term. A computer on the node's network can still fetch them itself (`clients/clips-upload.py`, never over the SIM), the way node 1's were fetched before the node sent them on its own. `LIFE_KEEP_CLIPS=0` keeps no clips at all. It is what keeps the node honest with the people whose land it sits on, and what keeps a 500 MB SIM alive for ten years. BirdNET-Go's clip saving is off in the image and only `life-clips` switches it on, for the sample.

The order of work for building one is `/docs/build`. Before a node is built: **both soil probes leave the factory on Modbus address 1**, so on one bus they collide and neither answers. On the bench, with only that probe connected:

```
life-soil-agent --scan                      # who answers, and with what raw registers
life-soil-agent --set-address 2 --addr 1    # write register 0x07D0
life-soil-agent --scan                      # confirm, then wire both
```

`--scan` prints the raw words next to the decoded values, because the profiles here were written from datasheets: stable words with nonsense values mean the register map is wrong and not the probe. The SEN0600 carries moisture and temperature only, so it uses the `sen0600` profile, which reads two registers; `generic-thc` reads a third for EC that this probe has not got.

We build the image once ("golden image", `image/`), flash it per node, drop that node's `life-node.env` with its three device tokens on the boot partition, and ship the box provisioned. The landowner mounts the post and pushes the probes in.


## Specified, not yet built

Four things borrowed from [acoupi](https://github.com/acoupi/acoupi) after reading its source. We are not taking the dependency: it runs on Celery with a RabbitMQ broker, which suits a device that stays powered rather than one that boots twice a day in winter, and it is GPL-3.0 against this repository's Apache-2.0. The designs are worth having anyway.

1. **Queue in SQLite on the writable partition.** Replace `queue.jsonl` with a table holding the message, its creation time, and the server's response. Do not delete on success: a reading that was accepted and one that was never sent should still be distinguishable a month later.
2. *Built.* **Heartbeat with metrics**, hourly on the detections post (`life-heartbeat.py`): uptime, CPU temperature, free disk, unsent count, throttle flags, and the serving cell. Since 2026-10-10 also the node's power and condition: from the Witty Pi 4 over I2C its input voltage (`vinV`, the battery on a 12 V node), what the Pi draws (`piW`), the temperature inside the box (`boxTempC`) why it last acted (`wake`), the battery guard's two levels (`lowV`, `recoveryV`) and whether the last stop was the guard's (`lvOff`); from the Victron controller over VE.Direct, when the cable is in, `battV`, `battA`, `pvV`, `pvW`, `charge`, `mpptErr`, `loadOn`, `yieldTodayWh`, `yieldYdayWh`, `pvMaxTodayW`. About 250 bytes on a post that goes anyway. `life-heartbeat --power` prints just that part on the bench. The oracle opens *low battery* under 12.8 V, while the node still talks, and when a node says on its way down that the guard stopped it (`wake` = `low-voltage`); 13.2 V closes it. Silent and struggling are different failures, and so is moved.
3. *Built (2026-10-07).* **Dawn and dusk on the node.** `life-schedule-wpi` uses `astral` with the place's coordinates to write the day's windows to the Witty Pi at every boot, instead of a nightly cron rewrite of an absolute-time schedule.
4. **Bounded sending.** Oldest first, with a cap per run, so a node returning from a week offline does not spend a month of SIM budget in one afternoon.
